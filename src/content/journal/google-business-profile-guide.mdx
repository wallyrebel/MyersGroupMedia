Search for almost any local business and Google shows a box with a map, a phone number, hours, photos and reviews. That box is a Google Business Profile. It is free, it takes an afternoon to do well, and most small businesses have barely touched theirs.

For many local companies, more people see the Google profile than the website and the Facebook page combined. It deserves your attention.

## First, claim it

Search your business name on Google. If a listing already exists, look for a link that says "Own this business?" and follow the steps. If nothing exists, create one at google.com/business. Google will verify that you are the real owner, usually by phone, video or a mailed postcard.

If someone else claimed your listing years ago — a former employee, an old marketing company — it can be recovered. It takes some patience, and it is worth it.

## Fill out every single field

A complete profile outperforms a half-finished one. Go through all of it:

- **Business name** — your real name, exactly as it appears on your sign. Do not stuff extra keywords into it; Google can suspend listings for that
- **Primary category** — the most important choice you will make. Pick the one that best describes what you mainly do
- **Additional categories** — add the others that genuinely apply
- **Hours** — including holiday hours. Wrong hours are a top cause of bad reviews
- **Phone number and website**
- **Services or products** — list them all, with short descriptions
- **Service area** — if you travel to customers, list the towns and counties you cover
- **Description** — a few plain sentences about what you do and where

## Add real photos, and keep adding them

Profiles with good photos get more calls and more requests for directions. Use real pictures: your storefront, your team, your trucks, your finished work. Skip the stock photos. Add a few new ones each month so the profile looks alive.

## Reviews are the engine

Reviews do two jobs at once. They help you rank, and they persuade the customer reading them. A simple routine:

1. **Ask every happy customer.** In person, right when they are pleased
2. **Make it easy.** Your profile gives you a short review link. Text it to them
3. **Reply to every review.** Thank the good ones. Answer the bad ones calmly and briefly, and offer to make it right. Future customers read your replies

One thing never to do: buy reviews, or offer discounts in exchange for them. It is against Google's rules, and it can get your listing penalized.

## Post updates

Your profile lets you post updates, offers and events, much like social media. A short post every week or two, with a photo, signals that the business is active and gives searchers a reason to choose you.

## Answer the questions

People can ask questions right on your profile, and anyone can answer them. Check regularly and answer them yourself, before a stranger does it wrong.

## Connect it to a real website

Your profile and your website back each other up. Google compares the two. When your website clearly lists the same name, phone number, services and service area as your profile, Google trusts both more. A profile pointing at a slow, outdated site, or at nothing, is leaving rankings on the table.

## A fifteen-minute checkup

Right now, search for your business on your phone and ask:

- Are the hours right?
- Is the phone number right?
- Is there a photo from the last three months?
- Have you replied to your most recent reviews?
- Does the listing say everything you do?

If any answer is no, you have just found free customers.
