Every day, people in your town type what you sell into Google. "Tire shop Ripley MS." "Dentist near me." "Roof repair New Albany." The businesses that show up get the call. The rest never know the search happened.

Getting your business to show up for those searches is called local SEO. It is not magic, and it is not a secret. It is a short list of things done well and kept up.

## How Google decides who to show

For local searches, Google weighs three things:

- **Relevance** — does this business do what the person is looking for?
- **Distance** — how close is it to the person searching, or to the town they named?
- **Prominence** — how well known and well reviewed is it?

You cannot move your building. But you can make it very clear what you do, where you do it, and that real customers trust you. That is the whole job.

## Step 1: Claim your Google Business Profile

The map results at the top of a local search come from Google Business Profiles. Yours is free, and it is the single most valuable thing a local business can set up. Fill out everything: categories, hours, services, service area, photos and a real description.

We wrote a full guide to [getting more from your Google Business Profile](/journal/google-business-profile-guide/).

## Step 2: Have a website that says where you work

Google reads your website to understand what you do and which towns you serve. A common mistake is a site that says "serving the area" without ever naming the area.

Say it plainly. If you serve Booneville, Baldwyn and the rest of Prentiss County, your website should say so in words. If you offer five services, each one deserves its own page.

## Step 3: Keep your name, address and phone number identical everywhere

Your business name, address and phone number should match exactly on your website, your Google profile, Facebook and every directory. "Main St." in one place and "Main Street, Suite B" in another, or an old phone number still floating around, makes Google less sure about you — and it sends customers to the wrong place.

## Step 4: Ask for reviews, every time

Reviews are one of the strongest local signals there is, and they are what customers read before they pick up the phone. The businesses with the most reviews are rarely the best in town. They are the ones that ask.

Make it a habit: when a customer is happy, ask them, and text them the link. Then reply to every review, good or bad.

## Step 5: Make your site fast and phone-friendly

Most local searches happen on a phone, often from a parking lot or a truck. If your site is slow or hard to use on a small screen, people leave, and Google notices. A fast, [mobile-friendly website](/services/web-design/) is part of SEO, not separate from it.

## Step 6: Answer the questions customers ask

Think about what people ask you on the phone. How much does it cost? How long does it take? Do you come to Iuka? Write those answers on your website. Helpful pages that answer real questions are what Google wants to show, and they are what turn a visitor into a customer.

## How long does local SEO take?

Fixing your Google profile can help within weeks. Ranking a website for competitive searches takes months of steady work. Smaller towns move faster than big markets like Tupelo or DeSoto County, simply because fewer businesses are competing.

The key word is steady. Local SEO is not something you do once. The businesses at the top keep at it.
