It is the question we hear most from business owners across North Mississippi: "I have a Facebook page and people know me. Do I really need a website?"

Short answer: yes. Longer answer: yes, and probably for different reasons than you think.

## Customers look you up before they call

Even when someone hears about you from a friend, the next thing they do is pull out their phone and search your name. They want to see your hours, your phone number, what you charge, photos of your work and whether you look like a real, established business.

If nothing comes up — or what comes up is a half-finished listing and a Facebook page that has not been posted to since spring — a lot of those people quietly move on to the next name. You never find out you lost them.

## Google cannot recommend a business it cannot read

When someone in Corinth searches "plumber near me" or someone in Tupelo searches "best barbecue," Google has to decide who to show. It makes that decision by reading websites. A site that clearly says what you do and where you do it gives Google something to work with.

No website means you are relying entirely on your Google Business Profile and luck. A website and a profile working together is how local businesses [show up in search](/services/digital-marketing/).

## You do not own your Facebook page

Facebook is a great tool, and we manage [social media](/services/social-media-marketing/) for plenty of businesses. But it is rented land. Facebook decides who sees your posts, and that number has dropped year after year. Accounts get hacked, locked and disabled every day, sometimes with no way to reach a person.

Your website is the one place online that belongs to you. Nobody can change the rules on you, and nobody can take it away.

## A website works when you are closed

Your website answers the same ten questions you answer on the phone all day — hours, location, services, service area, pricing, how to book — at ten at night and on Sunday afternoon. For a small business, that is like having an employee who never clocks out.

## It makes you look as good as you are

Fair or not, people judge a business by how it looks online. A clean, modern, easy-to-use website tells customers you are professional and here to stay. An outdated one, or none at all, suggests the opposite, no matter how good your work actually is.

## "But I already have all the work I can handle"

That is a good problem. A website still helps, in two ways:

- **Better customers.** When more people are asking for you, you get to choose the jobs you want and charge what you are worth.
- **Insurance.** Word of mouth can dry up when a big customer leaves, a competitor opens or the economy turns. A website that already ranks is a safety net you cannot build overnight.

## What a small business website actually needs

You do not need fifty pages or anything fancy. You need:

1. **Your name, phone number and service area** at the top of every page
2. **A clear list of what you do**, in plain words
3. **Real photos** of your work, your team or your place
4. **A way to contact you in one tap** — call or email
5. **Speed.** If it takes more than a few seconds to load on a phone, people leave
6. **The basics of SEO** built in from the start
