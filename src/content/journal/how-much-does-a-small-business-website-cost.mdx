Ask five people what a website costs and you will get five answers, from "it is free" to a number that makes you sit down. They can all be right, because "a website" can mean very different things.

Here is what you are actually paying for, so you can tell a fair quote from a bad one.

## The three ways to get a website

### 1. Build it yourself

Do-it-yourself website builders charge a monthly fee and give you templates to fill in.

**Good for:** Brand-new businesses with more time than money.

**The catch:** Your time is the real cost. Most owners spend many evenings on it and end up with something that looks like a template, loads slowly and does not rank. And the monthly fee never ends.

### 2. Hire the cheapest option you can find

There is always someone who will build a site for next to nothing.

**The catch:** You often get a recycled template, no search engine work, and a builder who disappears. We are regularly asked to rescue these sites, and the owner ends up paying twice.

### 3. Hire a professional

A professional designs the site around your business, writes or polishes the content, builds in the SEO foundation, launches it, and is still there afterward.

**The catch:** It costs more up front. Done right, it is the one that pays for itself.

## What drives the price

When we quote a website, these are the things that move the number:

- **Number of pages.** A five-page site is less work than a fifty-page one
- **Custom design.** A design made for your business takes longer than a template with your logo on it
- **Content.** Do you have the text and photos, or do they need to be written and taken?
- **Features.** Online ordering, booking, payments, customer logins and galleries all add work
- **Online store.** Selling products online is a bigger project than a brochure site
- **SEO work.** Individual pages for each service and each town you serve take time, and they are what get you found
- **Moving an old site.** Carrying over content and protecting the rankings you already have

## The costs people forget

The build is one cost. Keeping a site running involves a few more:

- **Domain name** — your web address, renewed every year
- **Hosting** — where the site lives. See our [hosting service](/services/web-hosting/)
- **Maintenance** — updates, security and backups
- **Changes** — new photos, new hours, new pages

Ask about every one of these before you sign anything. A low build price with high monthly fees is not a bargain.

## Questions to ask before you hire anyone

1. **Do I own the website and the domain name?** The answer should be yes, in writing
2. **What exactly is included?** Pages, design rounds, content, SEO
3. **What will I pay each month after launch, and what does it cover?**
4. **Can I make simple updates myself?**
5. **Who do I call when something breaks?**
6. **Can I see sites you have built for businesses like mine?**

If any answer is vague, keep looking.

## Is it worth it?

Think of it this way. What is one new customer worth to your business? For a roofer, a dentist or an attorney, a single job can pay for the whole site. For a restaurant or a shop, it is a steady stream of new faces over years.

A website is not a cost like the light bill. It is closer to a sign on the busiest road in town, one that works around the clock.
