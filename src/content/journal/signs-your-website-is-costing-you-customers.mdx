Most business owners think of an old website as harmless. It is there, it has the phone number on it, it is fine.

It is not fine. A bad website actively loses you business, because it is often the first impression a customer gets. Here are seven signs yours is working against you. You can check every one of them from your own phone in ten minutes.

## 1. It takes more than a few seconds to load

Pull up your site on your phone, on cellular data, not Wi-Fi. Count. If you get past three and you are still waiting, a good share of your visitors have already hit the back button. Slow sites also rank lower on Google.

**The fix:** Compress oversized photos, remove the clutter, and put the site on [faster hosting](/services/web-hosting/). Sometimes a rebuild is cheaper than patching.

## 2. It is hard to use on a phone

Do you have to pinch and zoom to read it? Are the buttons too small to tap? Does the menu cover the screen? Most local searches happen on phones. A site that only works on a desktop computer is turning away most of its visitors.

**The fix:** A mobile-first design, built for a thumb before it is built for a mouse.

## 3. The phone number is hard to find

Your phone number should be at the top of every page, and tapping it should start a call. If a customer has to hunt for a "Contact" page to reach you, many will not bother.

**The fix:** A tap-to-call number in the header, and clear contact buttons on every page.

## 4. It says "Not secure" in the address bar

If your web address starts with http instead of https, browsers warn visitors that your site is not secure. That is a frightening message to see next to a business name, and it is completely avoidable.

**The fix:** An SSL certificate, which any decent host should include and renew for you.

## 5. The information is out of date

Old hours. A promotion from two summers ago. Staff who left. A copyright date that is five years old. Customers notice, and they wonder what else is not being kept up.

**The fix:** A site you can easily update yourself — or a team that will update it for you the same week you ask.

## 6. It does not show up on Google

Search for what you do plus your town, the way a stranger would. Not your business name — your service. "Flooring store Pontotoc." "Tax preparer Holly Springs." If you are not on the first page, most searchers will never see you.

**The fix:** [Local SEO](/services/digital-marketing/): a complete Google Business Profile, pages that clearly name your services and your service area, and steady reviews. Our [local SEO guide](/journal/local-seo-guide-north-mississippi/) walks through it.

## 7. It does not look like your business anymore

Maybe you built it yourself ten years ago. Maybe a nephew did. Maybe it was fine at the time. But if your work is professional and your website looks like an afterthought, customers will believe the website.

**The fix:** A fresh design with real photos of your real work and a [brand](/services/graphic-design/) that matches your sign, your trucks and your cards.

## How many did you count?

- **One or two:** A tune-up will probably do it
- **Three or four:** You are losing customers every week. Time to plan a rebuild
- **Five or more:** Your website is hurting more than it is helping

## What a good website looks like

It loads fast. It works on a phone. It says what you do and where, in plain words. It makes calling or emailing a one-tap job. And it shows up when people search.
