In a lot of North Mississippi towns, Facebook is the community bulletin board. It is where people share specials, ball scores, yard sales and recommendations. So it is no surprise that many local businesses run entirely on a Facebook page.

It works, up to a point. Here is where that point is, and why the strongest local businesses have both a Facebook page and a website.

## What Facebook does well

- **Staying in front of people who already know you.** Specials, new arrivals, closings and photos of today's work
- **Conversation.** Comments, messages and shares
- **Community.** People tag a friend and say "y'all need to try this place"
- **Targeted ads.** You can put a post in front of people in specific towns

Facebook is excellent at keeping existing customers engaged. We build and manage [social media](/services/social-media-marketing/) for exactly that reason.

## What Facebook does badly

### It does not show up when strangers search

When someone new to town searches Google for "electrician near me," Google shows websites and Google Business Profiles. Facebook pages rarely appear, and when they do, they are far down the list. If your only presence is a Facebook page, you are close to invisible to anyone who does not already know your name.

### Most of your followers never see your posts

A typical post reaches only a small share of the people who follow the page. Facebook would like you to pay to reach the rest.

### Information gets buried

Try finding a business's prices, service area or holiday hours on a Facebook page. You scroll, you guess and you give up. A website puts the important things where people expect to find them.

### Not everyone is on it

A growing number of customers, younger ones especially, do not use Facebook at all. And some people will not log in to see your page.

### It is not yours

This is the big one. Facebook can change its rules, shrink your reach or lock your account at any time. Pages get hacked and disabled every day, and getting one back can take weeks, if it happens at all. Building a business only on Facebook is like building a house on land you rent month to month.

## What a website does that Facebook cannot

- **Gets found on Google** by people who have never heard of you
- **Answers questions fast** — hours, services, service area, contact — without scrolling
- **Looks professional** and builds trust before the first call
- **Belongs to you**, on your own domain name
- **Lasts.** A good page keeps bringing in customers for years. A Facebook post is gone in a day

## How they work together

Think of it this way: **Facebook is where you talk. Your website is where you close.**

- Post on Facebook, and link to your website for the details
- Let your website collect the calls and emails
- Use your website's content as material for your posts
- Put your Facebook link on your website so new visitors can follow you

Each one makes the other stronger.

## "Which one first?"

If you have neither, start with a simple website and a Google Business Profile, because that is what gets you found by new customers. Then add Facebook to keep them.

If you already have a busy Facebook page, you are ahead of most. Now give all of that attention a home that you own.
